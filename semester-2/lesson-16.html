Iterating and Improving the Project

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 9 · Testing & Iteration Iteration Cycle

Why This Matters

No project is perfect the first time. Iteration means repeating the cycle of improving, building, testing, and getting feedback. Each iteration makes your project better. This is how real software is made.

What You Will Learn

  • Apply improvements based on feedback.
  • Re-test the improved system.
  • Document the iteration process.

Words to Know

iteration improvement version change log re-test cycle

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — What is Iteration? 0–10 min

Ask yourself: What does iteration mean?

Repeating a process to improve.

Iteration makes your project better over time.

Part 2 — The Iteration Cycle 10–30 min

The cycle:

  1. Plan improvements.
  2. Build changes.
  3. Test.
  4. Get feedback.
  5. Repeat.

Documentation:

  • Version number (v1.0, v1.1).
  • Change log.
  • What changed and why.

Part 3 — Apply Improvements Together 30–55 min

Apply 3 improvements to the library system.

Update the change log.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Iterate Your Project 55–85 min

Do this in your project:

  • Apply 5 improvements.
  • Re-test.
  • Update the change log.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Share your improvements.

Discuss: What improved the most?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

Describe one improvement and its effect.

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can iterate and document improvements.

Support: Use an iteration template.

Challenge: Run multiple iterations.

Complete your iteration and prepare for the final phase.
