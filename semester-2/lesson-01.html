100 minutes Phase 6 · Data Modeling & Database Design Tables, Fields, Records

Why This Matters

Every project needs to store data — books, users, orders, sensor readings. Data modeling is how you organize that data before you build. Think of it like labelling jars on a shelf: when everything has its place, you can find it instantly.

What You Will Learn

  • Define data modeling.
  • Identify tables, fields, and records.
  • Create a data model for your project.

Words to Know

data model table field record column row schema data type

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — How Do You Store Data? 0–10 min

Ask yourself: How do you store data in your project?

  • Lists
  • Tables
  • Files
  • Databases

Data modeling organizes data before you build.

Part 2 — Tables, Fields, Records 10–30 min

Learn the key terms:

  • Table: a collection of related data (Books).
  • Field: a column (Title, Author, Genre).
  • Record: a row (one book).
  • Data type: text, number, date, boolean.

Example table:

  • BookID | Title | Author | Genre | Available
  • 1 | Harry Potter | J.K. Rowling | Fantasy | Yes
  • 2 | The Hobbit | J.R.R. Tolkien | Fantasy | No

Part 3 — Create a Data Model Together 30–55 min

Create tables for the library system:

  • Books.
  • Users.
  • Borrowing history.

Identify fields and data types.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Model Your Data 55–85 min

Create data models for your project:

  • At least 3 tables.
  • Fields and data types for each.
  • Sample records.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Present your data model.

Discuss: What is the most important table in your project?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

List 3 tables and 3 fields for your project.

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can create a data model with tables and fields.

Support: Use a data model template.

Challenge: Add validation rules.
