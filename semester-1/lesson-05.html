Semester 1 · Lesson 5

Observing and Identifying Real Problems

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 2 · Problem Identification & Scoping Needs Assessment, Observation

Why This Matters

Every meaningful project starts with a real problem. But problems are not always obvious — sometimes what we see is only a symptom, not the real cause. Learning to observe carefully and dig deeper is the first step to building something that actually helps people.

What You Will Learn

  • Observe real-world problems in your community.
  • Tell the difference between symptoms and root causes.
  • Create a list of potential project problems.

Words to Know

observation symptom root cause needs assessment community stakeholder

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — What Problems Do You See? 0–10 min

Look around your school or community. What problems do you notice?

  • Long queues at the canteen
  • Food waste
  • Water waste
  • Traffic at the gate
  • Lost library books

Good projects start with real problems.

Part 2 — Symptoms vs Root Causes 10–30 min

A symptom is what you see. A root cause is why it happens.

Example:

  • "Students are late to class" (symptom)
  • "The gate is crowded at 8:00" (cause)
  • "Traffic flow is poorly designed" (root cause)

Use the 5 Whys method to dig deeper:

  1. Why are students late? Because the gate is crowded.
  2. Why is the gate crowded? Because there is only one entrance.
  3. Why is there only one entrance? Because the school was built for fewer students.

Projects should solve root causes, not symptoms.

Part 3 — Practise Together: Food Waste 30–55 min

Let's analyse "Food waste in the cafeteria" together using the 5 Whys:

  1. Why is there food waste? Students take too much.
  2. Why do they take too much? Portions are not measured.
  3. Why are portions not measured? No system for portion control.
  4. Why is there no system? No data on how much students eat.
  5. Why no data? No tracking system.

Root cause: lack of a tracking and portioning system.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Problem Observation 55–85 min

Observe your domain:

  • Health, agriculture, energy, environment, library, trade, food.

Create a problem list:

  • At least 5 problems.
  • For each, identify the symptom and root cause.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Share your top problem.

Discuss: Which problem is most worth solving?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

Write one symptom and its root cause for a problem in your domain.

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can identify problems and root causes.

Support: Use a 5 Whys template.

Challenge: Interview a stakeholder.

Before Next Lesson
