Data Flow Diagrams and Process Modeling

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 4 · System Design & Requirements Data Flow Modeling

Why This Matters

Data moves through your system like water through a river. It enters from users, flows through processes, gets stored, and comes back out as information. A Data Flow Diagram (DFD) helps you see this movement clearly.

What You Will Learn

  • Create a data flow diagram (DFD).
  • Model the processes in your system.
  • Identify data inputs, processes, and outputs.

Words to Know

data flow diagram process input output data store external entity DFD level 0 DFD level 1

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — How Does Data Move? 0–10 min

Ask yourself: How does data move through your system?

  • Input → Process → Storage → Output.

Data flow diagrams show how data moves.

Part 2 — DFD Symbols and Levels 10–30 min

Learn the symbols:

  • External entity (rectangle): user, system.
  • Process (circle): action.
  • Data store (open rectangle): database, file.
  • Data flow (arrow): movement of data.

Learn the levels:

  • Level 0: the whole system as one process.
  • Level 1: the main processes inside.
  • Level 2: details of each process.

Example for the library system:

  • Level 0: User → Library System → Database.
  • Level 1: Search, Recommend, Borrow, Return.

Part 3 — Draw a DFD Together 30–55 min

Draw a Level 1 DFD for the library system.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Create Your DFD 55–85 min

Create:

  • Level 0 DFD.
  • Level 1 DFD.
  • At least one Level 2 DFD.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Present your DFD.

Discuss: Where does data get stored in your system?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

Draw a simple DFD for one process in your system.

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can create DFDs at multiple levels.

Support: Use a DFD template.

Challenge: Add error flows.
