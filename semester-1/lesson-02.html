Decomposition in Real-World Projects

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 1 · Computational Thinking Foundations Breaking Complex Problems into Sub-Systems

Why This Matters

Big problems feel impossible — until you break them down. A fern looks like one big leaf, but it is actually hundreds of tiny leaflets arranged in a pattern. In the same way, a project that seems overwhelming becomes manageable when you split it into smaller pieces.

That skill is called decomposition, and it is one of the most powerful tools in computational thinking.

What You Will Learn

  • Break a project into major sub-systems.
  • Break each sub-system into smaller tasks.
  • Draw a decomposition tree for your own project.

Words to Know

decomposition sub-system module component task tree diagram hierarchy

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — One Task or Many? 0–10 min

Imagine your teacher asks you to "build a health care system."

Is that one task? No — it is many. Just look at what it includes:

  • Patient records
  • Appointments
  • Reminders
  • Reports
  • User login
  • Data security

Decomposition is the skill of breaking a project into manageable sub-systems.

Part 2 — Multi-Level Decomposition 10–30 min

You can break down a project in levels:

  • Level 0: The whole project.
  • Level 1: Major sub-systems.
  • Level 2: Tasks inside each sub-system.
  • Level 3: Detailed steps inside a task (if needed).

Example — Automatic plant control system:

  • Level 1: Sensor module, decision logic, pump control, user interface, data logging.
  • Level 2 for Sensor module: choose sensor, connect to controller, read data, calibrate.
  • Level 3 for Calibrate: test dry soil, test wet soil, set threshold.

Part 3 — Practise Together: The Library System 30–55 min

Let's decompose the interactive library recommendation system as a class:

  • Level 1: Book database, user profiles, recommendation engine, search interface, borrowing history.
  • Level 2 for Recommendation engine: collect user preferences, match to books, rank results, display suggestions.
  • Level 3 for Match to books: filter by genre, filter by reading level, sort by popularity.

Draw the tree on the board together.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Decompose Your Project 55–85 min

In your group, decompose your chosen project:

  • At least 5 sub-systems (Level 1).
  • At least 3 tasks per sub-system (Level 2).
  • At least 1 task broken into steps (Level 3).

Draw the tree on large paper.

Ask yourself: Can this be broken down further?

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Present your tree to the class.

Discuss: Which sub-system is most complex? Why?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

What is the most important sub-system in your project? Why?

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can decompose a project into at least 3 levels.

Support: Use a partially completed tree template.

Challenge: Can you identify dependencies between sub-systems?
