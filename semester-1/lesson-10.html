Semester 1 · Lesson 10

Timeline, Milestones, and Task Breakdown

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 3 · Project Planning & Management Gantt Chart, Milestones

Why This Matters

Projects often fail because there is no plan. A timeline keeps your team on track, helps you see what comes next, and makes sure you finish on time. In this lesson, you will create a 12-week timeline for your project with clear milestones and deliverables.

What You Will Learn

  • Create a project timeline.
  • Define milestones and deliverables.
  • Break tasks into weekly goals.

Words to Know

timeline milestone deliverable task Gantt chart deadline dependency

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — Why Do Projects Fail? 0–10 min

Ask yourself: Why do projects often fail or run late?

  • No plan
  • Unclear tasks
  • Missed deadlines

A timeline keeps a project on track.

Part 2 — Timelines and Milestones 10–30 min

Learn the key terms:

  • Timeline: a visual plan of when tasks happen.
  • Milestone: a key checkpoint (e.g., "prototype ready").
  • Deliverable: something you produce (e.g., report, code, diagram).
  • Dependency: a task that must be done before another.

Example Gantt chart for the library system:

  • Weeks 1–2: user assessment.
  • Weeks 3–4: database design.
  • Weeks 5–6: recommendation engine.
  • Weeks 7–8: interface.
  • Weeks 9–10: testing.
  • Weeks 11–12: presentation.

Part 3 — Build a Timeline Together 30–55 min

Create a 12-week timeline for the library system project.

Include milestones and deliverables.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Create Your Timeline 55–85 min

Create a 12-week timeline for your project:

  • Weekly tasks.
  • Milestones.
  • Deliverables.
  • Dependencies.

Create a Gantt chart in Google Sheets.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Present your timeline.

Discuss: What is your biggest risk for running late?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

What is the first milestone in your project?
