Writing Problem Statements and Project Goals

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 2 · Problem Identification & Scoping Clear Problem Statement, Goals

Why This Matters

A problem statement is like the seed of your project. If the seed is strong and clear, the project will grow well. If it is vague, the project will struggle. In this lesson, you will learn how to write a specific, measurable, user-focused problem statement and set clear goals for your project.

What You Will Learn

  • Write a clear problem statement.
  • Define project goals and success criteria.
  • Align your project with a real-life need.

Words to Know

problem statement goal success criteria scope stakeholder impact

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — What Makes a Good Problem Statement? 0–10 min

Compare these two statements:

  • "Our school has problems."
  • "Students waste 30% of cafeteria food because there is no portion tracking system."

Which is clearer? Why?

A problem statement is specific, measurable, and user-focused.

Part 2 — Writing Problem Statements 10–30 min

Use this structure:

  • Who: the user or group affected.
  • What: the problem.
  • Where/When: the context.
  • Why it matters: the impact.
  • How we know: the evidence.

Example:

  • Who: Students and cafeteria staff.
  • What: 30% of food is wasted daily.
  • Where/When: At lunch, every school day.
  • Why: It costs money and harms the environment.
  • Evidence: Waste audit shows 15 kg per day.

Set goals that are measurable and time-bound:

  • Goal: Reduce food waste by 50% in 3 months.

Part 3 — Practise Together: Library System 30–55 min

Write a problem statement together:

  • Who: Students and librarians.
  • What: Finding books takes too long.
  • Where/When: At the library, during free periods.
  • Why: Students avoid the library.
  • Evidence: 60% of students say they can't find what they want.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Write Your Problem Statement 55–85 min

In your group, write:

  • Your problem statement.
  • Your project goal.
  • 3 success criteria.
  • An impact statement.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Present your problem statement.

Discuss: Is your problem statement specific and measurable?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

Write your project's problem statement in one sentence.

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can write a clear, measurable problem statement.

Support: Use a fill-in-the-blank template.

Challenge: Include a theory of change.
