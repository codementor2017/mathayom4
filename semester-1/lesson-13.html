Functional and Non-Functional Requirements

Previous Course Home Next Lesson
100 minutes Phase 4 · System Design & Requirements Requirements Document

Why This Matters

Before you build anything, you need to know exactly what your system should do and how well it should do it. Functional requirements say what the system does. Non-functional requirements say how well it does it. Together, they become your project's blueprint.

What You Will Learn

  • Distinguish functional and non-functional requirements.
  • Write requirements for your project.
  • Prioritize requirements.

Words to Know

requirement functional non-functional priority must-have nice-to-have constraint

Lesson Journey (100 minutes)

Part 1 — What Should Your System Do? 0–10 min

Ask yourself: What should your system do?

  • Search
  • Recommend
  • Display
  • Save
  • Alert

Ask: How well should it do it?

  • Fast
  • Easy
  • Secure
  • Reliable

Part 2 — Two Types of Requirements 10–30 min

Functional: what the system does (e.g., search books by title).

Non-functional: how well it does it (e.g., search results in under 1 second).

Prioritize:

  • Must-have: essential for the project.
  • Should-have: important but not critical.
  • Nice-to-have: optional.

Examples for the library system:

  • Functional: search by title, author, genre; recommend books; display availability.
  • Non-functional: search under 1 second; works on mobile; secure login.

Part 3 — Write Requirements Together 30–55 min

Write 10 requirements for the library system:

  • 6 functional.
  • 4 non-functional.
  • Prioritize each.

Part 4 — Your Turn: Write Your Requirements 55–85 min

Write:

  • 6+ functional requirements.
  • 4+ non-functional requirements.
  • Priority for each.

Part 5 — Share and Discuss 85–95 min

Present your requirements.

Discuss: Which requirement is most important? Why?

Part 6 — Exit Ticket 95–100 min

Write one functional and one non-functional requirement for your project.

Check Your Understanding

You are ready to move on when you can write and prioritize requirements.
